# INT335: Design Thinking
## Unit 1: Foundations of Learning, Creativity and Design Thinking (Quick Revision Guide)

---

### 1. Key Concept Overview
* **Design Thinking**: A human-centred, iterative problem-solving methodology that combines cognitive science, creative exploration, and empirical experimentation.
* **Governing Rule**: Always start with **human needs** rather than technology or predetermined solutions.
* **Wicked Problems**: Coined by Horst Rittel and Melvin Webber (1973); complex, ill-defined social/systemic problems with no definitive formulation and no single correct answer.
* **Three Lenses of Innovation (IDEO)**:
  1. **Desirability**: What do people need and want? (Human)
  2. **Feasibility**: What is technically possible? (Technology)
  3. **Viability**: What is financially and operationally sustainable? (Business)

---

### 2. Learning & Creative Thinking Dynamics
* **Memory & Retrieval**:
  * *Encoding & Active Retrieval*: Active recall builds stronger memory traces than passive re-reading.
  * *Prior Knowledge Schema*: New concepts attach to existing mental frameworks.
  * *Deliberate Practice*: Goal-oriented practice with immediate feedback and correction.
* **Thinking Modes**:
  * **Divergent Thinking**: Expanding possibilities, generating varied ideas (fluency and flexibility).
  * **Convergent Thinking**: Analyzing, filtering, and evaluating ideas against constraints.
  * *Productive Rhythm*: Creative problem solving alternates between divergence and convergence.
* **Brain Modes (Barbara Oakley)**:
  * **Focused Mode**: High concentration, logical step-by-step reasoning, error detection (limitation: can cause fixation).
  * **Diffuse Mode**: Relaxed attention, background incubation, spontaneous synthesis, big-picture connections (requires prior focused engagement).

---

### 3. Cognitive Frameworks & Mental Blocks
* **Kolb's Experiential Learning Cycle**:
  1. Concrete Experience $\rightarrow$ 2. Reflective Observation $\rightarrow$ 3. Abstract Conceptualization $\rightarrow$ 4. Active Experimentation.
* **Bloom's Revised Taxonomy**: Remember $\rightarrow$ Understand $\rightarrow$ Apply $\rightarrow$ Analyze $\rightarrow$ Evaluate $\rightarrow$ Create.
* **VARK Model**: Visual, Aural, Read/Write, Kinesthetic (modalities for engagement; not rigid innate limits).
* **Functional Fixedness**: Cognitive bias where an object is perceived only for its traditional use (Karl Duncker's 1945 Candle Problem).
* **Mental Sets & Cognitive Blocks**: Habitual problem-solving routines, premature judgment, and social conformity that prevent innovative exploration.

---

### 4. Creativity vs. Invention vs. Innovation
| Dimension | Definition | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Creativity** | Generating novel and appropriate ideas | Conceptualizing a low-water sanitation mechanism |
| **Invention** | Building a technically new device/process (patentable) | Creating a new micro-filtration membrane |
| **Innovation** | Implementing and scaling a solution that delivers real-world value | Distributing affordable clean water filters via rural entrepreneurship |

---

### 5. Historical Milestones & Process Frameworks
* **1919**: Bauhaus School (form follows function, multidisciplinary design).
* **1969**: Herbert Simon publishes *The Sciences of the Artificial* ("Design is transforming existing conditions into preferred ones").
* **1973**: Rittel & Webber introduce "Wicked Problems".
* **1987**: Peter Rowe publishes *Design Thinking*.
* **1991**: IDEO founded (popularized commercial human-centred design).
* **2005**: Stanford d.school established by David Kelley.
* **Stanford d.school 5-Stage Model**:
  1. **Empathize**: Understand user context, emotions, and latent needs.
  2. **Define**: Synthesize research into an actionable Point-of-View (POV) statement.
  3. **Ideate**: Generate wide ranges of solutions without premature judgment.
  4. **Prototype**: Build tangible, lightweight models to test specific questions.
  5. **Test**: Gather user feedback in context to refine solutions and assumptions.
* **IDEO 3I Framework**: **Inspiration** $\rightarrow$ **Ideation** $\rightarrow$ **Implementation**.

---

### 6. Landmark Global Case Studies
* **OXO Good Grips (1990)**: Universal design for kitchen peelers inspired by arthritis; universal usability created mass-market commercial dominance.
* **M-Pesa (Kenya, 2007)**: Mobile phone-based peer-to-peer micro-banking utilizing SMS and local agent networks, solving unbanked population needs.
* **Jaipur Foot (India)**: Low-cost, durable prosthetic limb customized for rural cultural activities (barefoot walking, squatting, farming).
* **Failure Cases**:
  * *Ford Edsel (1957)*: Mismatched market expectations, overpromoted styling, poor quality control.
  * *Google Glass (Consumer)*: Social friction, privacy anxieties, unclear everyday value proposition.
  * *Juicero (2017)*: Expensive over-engineering solving a non-existent user problem (packets could be hand-squeezed).

# INT335: Design Thinking
## Unit 2: Empathy, Observation and Problem Identification (Quick Revision Guide)

---

### 1. Key Concept Overview
* **Empathy**: The core pillar of Design Thinking; stepping into the user's shoes to understand their lived experiences, emotional states, constraints, and unmet needs.
* **Need vs. Solution**: A need is an outcome or state a user seeks (e.g., *"needs to reduce anxiety during train delays"*); a solution is a specific implementation (e.g., *"build a mobile app"*).
* **Computational Empathy**: Designing software and engineering systems to dynamically respond to user context, emotional states, cognitive load, and accessibility needs.

---

### 2. User Research Methods
* **Qualitative vs. Quantitative**:
  * *Qualitative*: Uncovers deep "Why" and "How" (Interviews, observation, diary studies; seeks rich thematic insight).
  * *Quantitative*: Measures "How much" and "How many" (Surveys, analytics, task logs; seeks statistical significance).
  * *Triangulation*: Comparing multiple data sources (e.g., combining analytics with observational field notes) to uncover true user reality.

---

### 3. Field Observation & The AEIOU Framework
Observing users in their natural environment without disrupting their workflow:
* **A — Activities**: Specific actions and tasks people perform (e.g., scanning items, searching menus).
* **E — Environments**: The physical or digital setting (lighting, noise, clutter, connectivity).
* **I — Interactions**: Relationships between person-to-person, person-to-device, or person-to-system.
* **O — Objects**: Physical and digital tools, workarounds, post-its, badges, devices used.
* **U — Users**: The people involved, their roles, behaviors, and relationships.

---

### 4. Effective Interviewing & Inquiry Techniques
* **Open-Ended Questions**: Ask *"Tell me about the last time you..."* rather than *"Do you like this feature?"*
* **Probing & Critical Incidents**: Dig into specific past stories of extreme frustration or delight.
* **Laddering (5 Whys)**: Progressively asking *"Why?"* to move from functional actions to deep core values and emotional drivers.
* **Avoiding Leading Questions**: Never frame questions that presume positive value (e.g., avoid *"How helpful was the app?"*).

---

### 5. Needs, Pain Points & Personas
* **Explicit Needs**: Directly verbalized requirements (e.g., *"I need an invoice download button"*).
* **Implicit / Latent Needs**: Unspoken expectations inferred from behaviors, hesitation, and workarounds (e.g., *need for audit trail reassurance and data privacy*).
* **Types of Pain Points**:
  1. *Time Pain*: Unnecessary delays, redundant steps.
  2. *Process Pain*: Switching between disjointed tools.
  3. *Financial Pain*: Hidden costs, unclear billing.
  4. *Emotional Pain*: Anxiety, frustration, embarrassment.
* **User Personas**: Research-grounded archetypes representing behavioral patterns, goals, motivations, frustrations, and technical context (avoid purely fictional stereotyping).

---

### 6. Empathy Mapping & Insights Synthesis
* **Empathy Map 4 Quadrants**:
  1. **Says**: Direct quotes and explicit statements.
  2. **Thinks**: Inferred beliefs, doubts, and worries.
  3. **Does**: Observable actions, physical behaviors, and workarounds.
  4. **Feels**: Emotional states (relief, confusion, urgency).
* **Pains & Gains**: Obstacles to remove vs. desired positive outcomes.
* **Actionable Insight**: Connecting evidence to underlying meaning:
  $$\text{User} + \text{Need} + \text{Surprising Insight}$$
* **HMW (How Might We)**: Framing open opportunity questions that spark ideation without dictating a solution.

# INT335: Design Thinking
## Unit 3: Ideation and Creative Problem Solving (Quick Revision Guide)

---

### 1. Key Concept Overview
* **Ideation**: The transition from understanding problems to generating candidate solutions.
* **Fundamental Rule**: **Separate idea generation (divergence) from idea evaluation (convergence)**.
* **Four Dimensions of Creative Ideas (Torrance Framework)**:
  1. **Fluency**: Total quantity of ideas produced.
  2. **Flexibility**: Diversity and range of categories covered.
  3. **Originality**: Uniqueness and novelty of concepts.
  4. **Elaboration**: Level of detail and development added.

---

### 2. Ideation Techniques & Tools
* **Brainstorming Rules (Alex Osborn)**:
  * Defer judgment / no early criticism.
  * Strive for volume (quantity breeds quality).
  * Encourage wild and unconventional ideas.
  * Build on the ideas of others (*"Yes, and..."*).
* **Brainwriting (6-3-5 Method)**: Silent generation on paper passed among teammates to eliminate dominant speaker bias.
* **SCAMPER Framework**:
  * **S** — Substitute (materials, components, steps)
  * **C** — Combine (integrate features or services)
  * **A** — Adapt (borrow solutions from other fields)
  * **M** — Modify / Magnify / Minify (change size, scale, speed)
  * **P** — Put to another use (repurpose existing assets)
  * **E** — Eliminate (remove complexity and redundant steps)
  * **R** — Reverse / Rearrange (invert sequences or user roles)
* **Mind Mapping**: Visual radial layout branching from a central challenge into associative themes.
* **Reverse Thinking (Inversion / Worst Possible Idea)**: Asking *"How could we make this problem worse?"* and inverting the answers to reveal hidden hazards and fresh solutions.

---

### 3. Organizing & Clustering Ideas
* **Affinity Diagramming**: Bottom-up clustering of ideas based on natural relationships and thematic patterns.
* **Concept Bundling**: Combining complementary small ideas into a cohesive, high-value solution package.

---

### 4. Feasibility, Screening & Prioritization
* **Multi-Criteria Evaluation**: Assessing ideas across Desirability, Technical Feasibility, and Business Viability.
* **Weighted Screening Matrix Formula**:
  $$T_i = \sum_{j=1}^n (w_j \times s_{ij})$$
  *(Where $w_j$ is criterion weight and $s_{ij}$ is the rating score)*.
* **Impact-Effort Matrix (2x2 Grid)**:
  * *High Impact, Low Effort*: **Quick Wins** (Implement first).
  * *High Impact, High Effort*: **Major Projects / Strategic Bets** (Plan carefully).
  * *Low Impact, Low Effort*: **Fill-ins / Minor Tweaks** (Do if time permits).
  * *Low Impact, High Effort*: **Thankless Tasks / Money Pits** (Drop/Avoid).
* **Voting Mechanisms**: Dot Voting (heat-mapping preferences) and token allocation.

## Unit 4: Product Design and Prototyping (Quick Revision Guide)

---

### 1. Key Concept Overview
* **Prototype**: A tangible, testable simulation or artifact created to answer specific questions, test hypotheses, and fail cheaply before production.
* **Prototyping Philosophy**: *"Build to think, test to learn."* Separate ego from the artifact.
* **Core Rule**: Test the **riskiest assumptions** first (e.g., willingness to use/trust before visual polish).

---

### 2. Prototype Fidelity Comparison
| Dimension | Low-Fidelity (Lo-Fi) | High-Fidelity (Hi-Fi) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Form** | Paper sketches, cardboard, wireframes | Clickable mockups, coded prototypes, 3D prints |
| **Speed & Cost** | Extremely fast and negligible cost | Slower, resource-intensive |
| **Primary Goal** | Test structural layout, concepts, and user flow | Test usability details, interactions, visual UI |
| **User Feedback** | Honest, conceptual feedback | Focuses on cosmetic and fine-grained UI details |

---

### 3. Paper Prototyping & Storyboarding
* **Paper Prototyping**:
  * Simulating screen workflows using paper cards, sticky notes, and cutouts.
  * *Roles*: User (acts on paper), "Computer/Facilitator" (swaps screens), Observer (notes errors silently).
* **Storyboarding**:
  * Comic-strip style sequential visualization of user experience over time.
  * *Key Elements*: User persona, trigger/context, action, interface interaction, emotional state, and resolution.

---

### 4. Wireframing Principles & UI Conventions
* **Wireframe**: A grayscale structural blueprint of a screen defining content hierarchy, UI controls, and layout.
* **Visual Restraint**: Use grayscale and basic typography to avoid premature debates on color/branding.
* **Standard Control Conventions**:
  * *Radio Buttons*: Select exactly **one** option from a mutually exclusive list.
  * *Checkboxes*: Select **multiple** independent options.
  * *Toggle Switches*: Immediate on/off binary setting.
  * *Buttons*: Trigger an action/submission.
* **Screen States**: Always design for default, empty, loading, error, and success states.

---

### 5. User Flows & Flowchart Conventions
* **User Flow**: A step-by-step path a user follows to complete a target task.
* **Standard Flowchart Symbols**:
  * *Oval (Terminator)*: Start / End of process.
  * *Rectangle*: Screen, Step, or User Action.
  * *Diamond*: Decision point (conditional branches: Yes/No).
  * *Arrow*: Directional flow.

---

### 6. Minimum Viable Product (MVP)
* **Definition (Eric Ries)**: The simplest version of a product that allows a team to collect the maximum amount of validated learning about customers with the least effort.
* **Key Characteristic**: Must deliver a complete, coherent slice of end-to-end value (not a half-built car, but a functional skateboard).
* **Types of MVPs**:
  * *Landing Page MVP*: Validates market demand and click-through interest before building.
  * *Concierge MVP*: Delivers the service manually behind the scenes to learn user behavior.
  * *Piecemeal MVP*: Assembles existing off-the-shelf tools into a working prototype.

## Unit 5: Testing, Validation and Customer Experience (Quick Revision Guide)

---

### 1. Key Concept Overview
* **Testing in Design Thinking**: An iterative learning mechanism to observe real behavior, uncover usability defects, and validate underlying problem-solution fit.
* **Usability Testing vs. Design Validation**:
  * *Usability Testing*: Checks if users can use the interface effectively, efficiently, and with satisfaction (*"Did we build the thing right?"*).
  * *Design Validation*: Checks if the product actually solves the right core user problem (*"Did we build the right thing?"*).

---

### 2. Conducting Neutral User Testing
* **"Show, Don't Tell" Principle**: Place the prototype in front of the user and observe natural interaction rather than pitching or explaining features.
* **Neutral Facilitation Rules**:
  * Use open, non-leading prompts (*"What would you expect to happen here?"*).
  * Clarify that the product is being tested, **not the user**.
  * Avoid defending design decisions or praising participant actions.
* **Task Scenario Design**:
  * Give realistic context and end goals without providing navigational steps or button names.
  * *Bad Task*: "Click the blue 'Pay' button to checkout."
  * *Good Task*: "You want to buy this book and have it delivered to your hostel by Friday. Complete your purchase."
* **Think-Aloud Protocol**: Asking participants to verbalize their thoughts, expectations, and hesitations as they perform tasks.

---

### 3. Feedback Capture Matrix
| Quadrant | Description | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Likes (+)** | What worked well, intuitive elements | "Clear order confirmation summary" |
| **Wishes ($\Delta$)** | Unmet needs, constructive critiques | "Wish I could edit address on the payment screen" |
| **Questions (?)** | Confusion, unanswered questions | "Does delivery include weekend shipping?" |
| **Ideas (!)** | New feature suggestions sparked during test | "Auto-save invoice to Google Drive" |

---

### 4. Usability vs. Customer Experience (CX)
* **Usability**: Interaction quality with a specific digital/physical interface (efficiency, error rate, task completion).
* **Customer Experience (CX)**: The cumulative, holistic perception across all touchpoints over time (discovery, buying, onboarding, customer support, returns, and brand trust).
* **Journey Mapping**: Visualizing end-to-end user experience across stages, identifying emotional highs, lows, touchpoints, and systemic pain points.
* **Touchpoints**:
  * *Human*: Support staff, delivery couriers.
  * *Digital*: Mobile app, email alerts, website.
  * *Physical*: Product box, paper receipt, retail store.

---

### 5. Digital Usability & Web Accessibility
* **Software Usability Pillars**: Clear navigation, system status visibility, user control (undo/cancel), error prevention, and immediate recovery.
* **Accessibility (WCAG POUR Principles)**:
  1. **P — Perceivable**: Information must be presentable to users in ways they can perceive (alt-text, captions).
  2. **O — Operable**: Interface components must be operable (full keyboard navigation, no traps).
  3. **U — Understandable**: Content and operation must be clear and predictable.
  4. **R — Robust**: Content must be interpretable across browsers and assistive technologies (screen readers).
* **Color Contrast Guidelines**: Minimum **4.5:1** contrast ratio for standard body text; **3:1** for large text.

## Unit 6: Innovation Project, Re-Design and Product Presentation (Quick Revision Guide)

---

### 1. Key Concept Overview
* **Innovation Process**: Iterative refinement connecting user research, design development, engineering feasibility, and persuasive communication.
* **The Refinement Cycle**:
  $$\text{Define Assumption} \rightarrow \text{Build Prototype} \rightarrow \text{Test with Users} \rightarrow \text{Record Evidence} \rightarrow \text{Refactor/Re-design}$$

---

### 2. Iteration, Refactoring & Strategic Pivoting
* **Incremental Iteration**: Small, localized design improvements (e.g., resizing touch targets, rewriting error text).
* **Design Refactoring**: Improving internal consistency, code modularity, UI component systems, or mechanical parts without altering core functionality.
* **Product Re-Design**: Fundamental structural overhaul triggered by invalidated problem definitions, architectural flaws, or major usability failures.
* **Strategic Pivot**: Changing a core pillar of the business/product model (e.g., target customer segment, value proposition, or delivery mechanism) while retaining validated learnings.
* **Feedback Prioritization Scoring**:
  $$P = F \times S \times C$$
  *(Where $F$ = Frequency, $S$ = Severity, $C$ = Confidence in evidence)*.
* **PDCA Cycle for Continuous Improvement**: **Plan** $\rightarrow$ **Do** $\rightarrow$ **Check** $\rightarrow$ **Act**.

---

### 3. Product Presentation & Communication Frameworks
* **Audience-Centred Communication**:
  * *Users*: Focus on usability, convenience, and direct benefits.
  * *Engineers*: Focus on technical architecture, feasibility, safety, and component specs.
  * *Investors / Executives*: Focus on market viability, unit economics, scalability, and ROI.
* **High-Impact Presentation Rules**:
  * Start with a compelling user hook or real-world problem evidence.
  * Convey **one clear core message**.
  * Use clean visual slides (one idea per slide, minimal clutter).
  * Show a live or recorded representative user task demo (*"Show, Don't Tell"*).
  * State limitations honestly to establish credibility.

---

### 4. The NABC Model (SRI International)
A powerful 4-part framework for pitching value propositions:
* **N — Need**: What is the critical, validated user pain point?
* **A — Approach**: What is the unique, feasible solution/mechanism?
* **B — Benefits per Costs**: What quantitative and qualitative value is delivered relative to the costs incurred?
* **C — Competition / Alternatives**: How does this outperform existing alternatives, manual workarounds, or non-consumption?

---

### 5. The Elevator Pitch
* **Definition**: A concise 30–60 second pitch delivering maximum clarity under time constraints.
* **Standard Structure**:
  > *"For **[target user]** who experiences **[critical problem]**, our **[product name]** is a **[product category]** that delivers **[key benefit]**. Unlike **[existing alternative]**, our solution **[key differentiator]**. Testing shows **[proven metric/evidence]**."*

---

### 6. Technical Case Study Deliverables
A complete design thinking project portfolio contains:
1. Empathy research findings & Problem Definition (POV / HMW).
2. Divergent ideation artifacts & Concept Selection Matrix.
3. Low and high-fidelity prototypes.
4. User test logs, Feedback Capture Matrices, and iteration history.
5. Final product specifications, system architecture, and viability plan.
