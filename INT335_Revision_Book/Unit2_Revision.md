# INT335: Design Thinking
## Unit 2: Empathy, Observation and Problem Identification (Quick Revision Guide)

---

### 1. Key Concept Overview
* **Empathy**: The core pillar of Design Thinking; stepping into the user's shoes to understand their lived experiences, emotional states, constraints, and unmet needs.
* **Need vs. Solution**: A need is an outcome or state a user seeks (e.g., *"needs to reduce anxiety during train delays"*); a solution is a specific implementation (e.g., *"build a mobile app"*).
* **Computational Empathy**: Designing software and engineering systems to dynamically respond to user context, emotional states, cognitive load, and accessibility needs.

---

### 2. User Research Methods
* **Qualitative vs. Quantitative**:
  * *Qualitative*: Uncovers deep "Why" and "How" (Interviews, observation, diary studies; seeks rich thematic insight).
  * *Quantitative*: Measures "How much" and "How many" (Surveys, analytics, task logs; seeks statistical significance).
  * *Triangulation*: Comparing multiple data sources (e.g., combining analytics with observational field notes) to uncover true user reality.

---

### 3. Field Observation & The AEIOU Framework
Observing users in their natural environment without disrupting their workflow:
* **A — Activities**: Specific actions and tasks people perform (e.g., scanning items, searching menus).
* **E — Environments**: The physical or digital setting (lighting, noise, clutter, connectivity).
* **I — Interactions**: Relationships between person-to-person, person-to-device, or person-to-system.
* **O — Objects**: Physical and digital tools, workarounds, post-its, badges, devices used.
* **U — Users**: The people involved, their roles, behaviors, and relationships.

---

### 4. Effective Interviewing & Inquiry Techniques
* **Open-Ended Questions**: Ask *"Tell me about the last time you..."* rather than *"Do you like this feature?"*
* **Probing & Critical Incidents**: Dig into specific past stories of extreme frustration or delight.
* **Laddering (5 Whys)**: Progressively asking *"Why?"* to move from functional actions to deep core values and emotional drivers.
* **Avoiding Leading Questions**: Never frame questions that presume positive value (e.g., avoid *"How helpful was the app?"*).

---

### 5. Needs, Pain Points & Personas
* **Explicit Needs**: Directly verbalized requirements (e.g., *"I need an invoice download button"*).
* **Implicit / Latent Needs**: Unspoken expectations inferred from behaviors, hesitation, and workarounds (e.g., *need for audit trail reassurance and data privacy*).
* **Types of Pain Points**:
  1. *Time Pain*: Unnecessary delays, redundant steps.
  2. *Process Pain*: Switching between disjointed tools.
  3. *Financial Pain*: Hidden costs, unclear billing.
  4. *Emotional Pain*: Anxiety, frustration, embarrassment.
* **User Personas**: Research-grounded archetypes representing behavioral patterns, goals, motivations, frustrations, and technical context (avoid purely fictional stereotyping).

---

### 6. Empathy Mapping & Insights Synthesis
* **Empathy Map 4 Quadrants**:
  1. **Says**: Direct quotes and explicit statements.
  2. **Thinks**: Inferred beliefs, doubts, and worries.
  3. **Does**: Observable actions, physical behaviors, and workarounds.
  4. **Feels**: Emotional states (relief, confusion, urgency).
* **Pains & Gains**: Obstacles to remove vs. desired positive outcomes.
* **Actionable Insight**: Connecting evidence to underlying meaning:
  $$\text{User} + \text{Need} + \text{Surprising Insight}$$
* **HMW (How Might We)**: Framing open opportunity questions that spark ideation without dictating a solution.
