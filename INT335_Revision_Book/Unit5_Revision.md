# INT335: Design Thinking
## Unit 5: Testing, Validation and Customer Experience (Quick Revision Guide)

---

### 1. Key Concept Overview
* **Testing in Design Thinking**: An iterative learning mechanism to observe real behavior, uncover usability defects, and validate underlying problem-solution fit.
* **Usability Testing vs. Design Validation**:
  * *Usability Testing*: Checks if users can use the interface effectively, efficiently, and with satisfaction (*"Did we build the thing right?"*).
  * *Design Validation*: Checks if the product actually solves the right core user problem (*"Did we build the right thing?"*).

---

### 2. Conducting Neutral User Testing
* **"Show, Don't Tell" Principle**: Place the prototype in front of the user and observe natural interaction rather than pitching or explaining features.
* **Neutral Facilitation Rules**:
  * Use open, non-leading prompts (*"What would you expect to happen here?"*).
  * Clarify that the product is being tested, **not the user**.
  * Avoid defending design decisions or praising participant actions.
* **Task Scenario Design**:
  * Give realistic context and end goals without providing navigational steps or button names.
  * *Bad Task*: "Click the blue 'Pay' button to checkout."
  * *Good Task*: "You want to buy this book and have it delivered to your hostel by Friday. Complete your purchase."
* **Think-Aloud Protocol**: Asking participants to verbalize their thoughts, expectations, and hesitations as they perform tasks.

---

### 3. Feedback Capture Matrix
| Quadrant | Description | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Likes (+)** | What worked well, intuitive elements | "Clear order confirmation summary" |
| **Wishes ($\Delta$)** | Unmet needs, constructive critiques | "Wish I could edit address on the payment screen" |
| **Questions (?)** | Confusion, unanswered questions | "Does delivery include weekend shipping?" |
| **Ideas (!)** | New feature suggestions sparked during test | "Auto-save invoice to Google Drive" |

---

### 4. Usability vs. Customer Experience (CX)
* **Usability**: Interaction quality with a specific digital/physical interface (efficiency, error rate, task completion).
* **Customer Experience (CX)**: The cumulative, holistic perception across all touchpoints over time (discovery, buying, onboarding, customer support, returns, and brand trust).
* **Journey Mapping**: Visualizing end-to-end user experience across stages, identifying emotional highs, lows, touchpoints, and systemic pain points.
* **Touchpoints**:
  * *Human*: Support staff, delivery couriers.
  * *Digital*: Mobile app, email alerts, website.
  * *Physical*: Product box, paper receipt, retail store.

---

### 5. Digital Usability & Web Accessibility
* **Software Usability Pillars**: Clear navigation, system status visibility, user control (undo/cancel), error prevention, and immediate recovery.
* **Accessibility (WCAG POUR Principles)**:
  1. **P — Perceivable**: Information must be presentable to users in ways they can perceive (alt-text, captions).
  2. **O — Operable**: Interface components must be operable (full keyboard navigation, no traps).
  3. **U — Understandable**: Content and operation must be clear and predictable.
  4. **R — Robust**: Content must be interpretable across browsers and assistive technologies (screen readers).
* **Color Contrast Guidelines**: Minimum **4.5:1** contrast ratio for standard body text; **3:1** for large text.
